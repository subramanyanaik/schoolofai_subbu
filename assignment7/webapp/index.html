The embedding that decodes back

Kronecker embeddings are forward deterministic. Make them reverse deterministic and the output head stops being a parameter — it becomes a derived, fixed, 0.019%-dense occupancy matrix. Vocabulary size stops costing anything.

“Kronecker is forward deterministic. How do I make a reverse of this? If we can do this, then we can get rid of the final head as well — then we can have a vocab of 1M as well without any issues.”

Both codecs run in your browser at identical D = 4096, same construction as src/kronecker_v2/codec.py. V2 decodes with an 8-bit sign read; V1 needs a 256-way search. Watch the nonzero counts — that gap is V1's sparsity waste, and it is why V1 must spend its 4096 dimensions on only 16 byte positions.

The mechanism

Why this one encoder makes the softmax cheap

and why a character CNN cannot

Character-CNN and compositional output heads already cut parameters. Neither cuts compute, because ⟨h, CNN(chars)⟩ has no structure — all |V| inner products must be formed.

The Kronecker codec is linear in byte-position occupancy, and no other surface-form encoder is. That single property changes the cost of the softmax:

logit(v) = ⟨S_pos, M_pos(v)⟩ = (1/√L_v) · Σ A[b_p, p] A = G · S_pos p<L_v A is one 256 × L_max table per batch. After it, every token's logit is a sum of L_v scalar lookups — no d-dimensional dot product exists.

Equivalently: the dense output matrix is replaced by the vocabulary's byte-occupancy matrix — fixed, derived, zero trainable parameters. And because it is a reformulation rather than an approximation, logits, the partition function and gradients come out bit-identical to the dense head. Unlike hierarchical, adaptive or sampled softmax, the distribution being trained does not change.

At matched D = 4096 V1 truncates 72 tokens and V2 truncates none — 8× the length limit at the same dimension. Length is not stored anywhere: it falls out of the decode, because the zero vector is reserved as “absent”. The zero byte stays a perfectly ordinary byte value.

A design claim that measurement killed

The original specification said overflow past L_max should alias rather than truncate — “graceful degradation”. It is the opposite of graceful.

truncatealias

Aliasing corrupts every position at once; truncation keeps a correct prefix. Truncation became the default, and the rejected alternative is still tested so the comparison stays reproducible.

Weight tying is free — in fact slightly better. A3 costs −0.0013 bpb and saves 1,572,864 parameters. Its entire head is 129 numbers. The base paper §3.5 calls tying architecturally inapplicable because D ≠ d_model; routing the head through the codec makes the mismatch irrelevant.

And KAS lands on the unigram floor — 2.4161 against 2.4025. The surface-form head learned almost nothing beyond token frequency.

The diagnosis, and the test of it

The codec's 1/√L normalisation makes every row of the output matrix exactly unit norm. A dense head encodes each token's unigram log-prior for free, in the magnitude of its row. KAS has no per-token magnitude at all — its only per-token freedom is 129 length-indexed values shared across 50,257 tokens.

So: add exactly one learned scalar per token, change nothing else.

E5 — capacity

D is the wrong quantity to reason about

seven configurations · 1,499,136 tokens each · comparable within this sweep only

The design-time claim was “D is the only capacity knob”. It is false. What predicts language-model quality is how close to mutually orthogonal the 256 byte codes are — and that has a closed form, so a codec can be configured analytically instead of by sweeping.

d_c=16 is degenerate — coherence 1.000 means some byte codes are exactly antipodal. Decoding still succeeds, because the sign separates them; the language model pays for the geometry anyway. And L_max should be as small as coverage allows: mean token length is 6.38 bytes, so high positions are almost always empty and extending L_max just spreads the codebook over cells that receive no gradient.

Strict monotonicity fails, and I am not rounding it up. There is one inversion, between the two best configurations, 0.0014 bpb apart at a 1.5M-token budget. That is inside noise and I have one seed — so the honest statistic is the rank correlation, not “perfect ordering”.

The sweep also says E4 above used the sixth-best configuration of seven. E4 was launched before this sweep existed. So the correction was tested.

What the correction is worth

The three KAS arms re-run at d_c=64, L_max=32 — same 5,001,216-token budget, same seed, same token stream. A0 and A1 are not re-run because d_c and L_max cannot affect them: A0 has no codec, and A1's V1 codec is pinned at d_c=256, L_max=16.

A 19.3M-parameter dense head has learned essentially nothing about Devanagari or Telugu byte sequences. The codec head sees the same bytes it always did. The fixed subspace that costs quality in-domain buys generalisation out-of-domain — one mechanism, two signs.

Watch A1 in particular: the V1 codec on the input side alone, with an ordinary dense head, already buys −12.9% on Hindi at no in-domain cost (+0.4% on English, perplexity 473.2 against A0's 472.2).

Confound, stated rather than glossed. A2 and A3 are much higher-entropy models (perplexity ~2265 vs A0's 472), and a flatter distribution is automatically better on out-of-distribution text. This design cannot separate byte-level transfer from calibration.

The reference run argued the effect was not only calibration because its tied arm beat the dense baseline on Spanish. In my run it does not — A3 is +4.5% on Spanish. So that argument does not replicate and the confound stands more strongly here. What survives is A1, which is not flat and still wins on Hindi. Treat the A2/A3/A4 magnitudes as an upper bound.

The sign flips on the rarest decile. This was the design-time prediction and it is what the data shows: frequent tokens need the unigram prior KAS cannot express; rare tokens need the surface-form generalisation the dense head never learns. Damage peaks in the mid-frequency band and reverses in the tail.

A4's whole curve sits below A2's — the unigram bias helps most exactly where the missing frequency prior hurt most — and it still wins slightly harder in the tail. The two mechanisms are separable, which is what the diagnosis predicted.

Limits

What this does not prove

This ran on an RTX 3050 Laptop with 4.3 GB against the reference run's 8.6 GB card, on FineWeb-Edu rather than CC-News, at a quarter of the token budget — 5,001,216 per arm against 20,174,848. Everything hardware-independent reproduced exactly: E1's 72 / 14 / 0 / 0 truncation counts, the 0.889 / 0.667 / 0.500 alias accuracies, the 38.6M → 3.15M parameter table, and Δparams == 0.

Everything budget-dependent moved, and in ways worth naming. A4 recovers about a third of the gap rather than 65%, because 50,257 fresh scalars in 2,442 optimizer steps barely move. E5's monotonicity gate fails. E7's confound is stronger. All language-model results are single-seed and directional; E1 and E2 carry the evidentiary weight, because they are theorems with machine-precision tests.

One apparent discrepancy turned out not to be budget at all. A2 sat slightly above the unigram floor here where the reference had it below — and that was a configuration artefact, resolved by the re-run above rather than explained away. E6, E7 and E8 were measured from the E4-config checkpoints and have not been re-derived at the better one; that is the next thing worth doing.

One bug worth recording

beta was originally sized from the vocabulary's observed longest token rather than from L_max. Every arm trained fine. The only symptom was that A2's parameter count crept by +33 between two vocabulary sizes — in a project whose entire claim is that the head's parameter count does not depend on |V|. The parameter audit failed, and it was right to.
